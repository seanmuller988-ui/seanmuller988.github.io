Evidence Extension · Planned

Forward evidence on a fixed configuration

Current state

Fitted parameters are out of sample in a walk-forward test; the choice among configurations is not. Data after the research window accrue with time, and the configuration is to be read on them without changes.

Open question

Whether the selected configuration, held fixed, behaves on later data as it does inside the two-year research window.

Why it matters

Selection happened inside the window, so only data arriving after the configuration is fixed is free of it. The months between the window’s end and that point are a weaker test.

Early hypothesis

A configuration chosen inside the window is probably flattered by it, so I do not read the headline as the expected result on later data. Over a few months the sampling error of an annualized Sharpe ratio is about as large as the ratio, so a short record tests consistency, not the level.

Planned approach

A planned extension is to apply the configuration, unchanged, to later data under its own evidence label. I am evaluating how to state in advance what would count as consistent.

What would change my view

Later-data trade frequency and win rate close to the backtest’s would be weak support. A clear departure would shift weight away from the backtest.

Robustness Research · Planned

From one compounding path to a distribution

Current state

The backtest headline is one compounding path, and a two-year multiple moves materially under small, arbitrary changes. Dependence-aware intervals, start-date sensitivity, attribution and leave-out tests were run on the first reported trade record and are being extended to the selected configuration as part of the continuing robustness program.

Open question

How wide the spread is across start dates, how much remains without the largest winners or after standard factors, and whether calendar dependence can be reduced without lowering the expected result.

Why it matters

One path is one draw. A right-tailed result should be shown as a property of the process, not of a few trades.

Early hypothesis

That the spread across starts comes from path dependence and a few large trades, not from model quality, as measurements on an earlier build showed.

Planned approach

I am evaluating shifted-start runs of the selected configuration. A planned extension is to repeat the leave-out tests and factor attribution on its trade record, with dependence-aware intervals.

What would change my view

A spread centered near the headline would support quoting it; one well below would mean quoting the spread instead. Profit is already concentrated in a few trades. If little remained without them, or standard factors absorbed most of the return, I would describe a concentrated or factor exposure, not a broad process.

Strategy Evolution · Planned

Regime coverage

Current state

The research window is about two years and covers one broad market environment: the S&P 500 rose about 42% over it on a price basis. Every backtest on this site sits inside that window, so the evidence describes those conditions and not materially different ones.

Open question

How the strategy behaves when market conditions differ materially from those in the research window, and how its design should adapt if they do.

Why it matters

Results from one environment cannot simply be extrapolated across structurally different periods.

Early hypothesis

Held loosely: adverse regimes may call for a separately researched component, not a re-tuning of the primary one.

Planned approach

A planned extension is to define regimes without using future information, then measure which components remain stable across them, on historical stress periods where data allows and against later observation. From there I am evaluating two responses: capital scaled by regime, and components specialized by regime in place of one configuration for all regimes.

What would change my view

Evidence that the components are stable across regimes would favor one configuration for all regimes over a separate component. The bar for any extension is that it improves robustness across market states without materially degrading results in the primary regime.

Execution and Capacity Research · Designing

Calibrating the impact model on the strategy’s own orders

Current state

The cost model’s impact coefficient comes from public tape data and a prior from the academic literature (execution page). Execution data from live operation are now being accumulated to refine and calibrate it on the strategy’s own orders.

Open question

How the accumulating fills should be benchmarked to measure the coefficient, and whether realized fills match the fill model at the same relative size.

Why it matters

The impact setting sits behind every modeled cost and capacity statement. An own-order estimate replaces an assumption with a measurement.

Early hypothesis

None beyond a convention I set: the model value stays the principal case until an own-order estimate exists.

Planned approach

One current research question is how to design the measurement: which benchmark price to compare fills against, and what range of order sizes to cover. A power analysis showed the benchmark largely determines the sample needed.

What would change my view

A measured coefficient well above the model value, or fills below the model’s, would raise modeled cost and lower every capacity statement. A value near the lower tape estimate would do the opposite.

Execution and Capacity Research · Planned

Cost, fills and capacity as simulated size grows

Current state

In the selected configuration’s historical backtest, modeled cost per round trip rises with the simulated starting balance; the execution page explains the mechanism and gives the $1M figure. The detailed execution and capacity studies ran on the first reported trade record, and the capacity study held profit per trade fixed.

Open question

How cost, fills and capacity behave as simulated size grows, and how much remains if profit per trade falls with size.

Why it matters

Earlier results do not carry over to the selected configuration. Capacity assuming undiminished profit per trade is an upper bound.

Early hypothesis

None on levels. In the earlier studies, at small size, the fill assumption acted through unfilled shares rather than cost. As size grows I expect the impact term, which rises with order size while the half-spread charge does not, to become the binding term.

Planned approach

A planned extension is to repeat the fill-assumption and fill-price stresses and shortfall decomposition on the selected configuration’s trade record, and to state capacity per signal, for the portfolio and against a marginal-return hurdle.

What would change my view

If central fill-price conventions, and not only an adverse bound on every fill, removed most of the modeled profit, the cost model would be the binding uncertainty.

Model Extension · Designing

What a second research sweep could detect

Current state

The October 2026 research sweep ended with one fragile survivor, an effect already known (case study). A feasibility study put the achievable skill for ranking individual signals below its pre-registered bar.

Open question

One current research question is whether learnable structure remains beyond the ranking of individual signals, and whether machine-learning methods can find leads on a sample this short without overfitting.

Why it matters

The first sweep showed that the sample, not the threshold, limited discovery. More of the same would add trials and noise, not findings.

Early hypothesis

Held loosely: these methods may surface leads that rule-based research misses, and on a two-year sample the risk of overfitting is as real as the potential.

Planned approach

I am investigating this in a separate research fork; nothing moves out of it without my item-by-item decision. I am evaluating how a second sweep should pose its questions so a realistic effect is detectable.

What would change my view

An effect that beats both its placebo and the effect already known would reopen that line of modeling. A second null from adequately powered studies would bound an effect, not show its absence.

Model Extension · Planned

The use of idle capital

Current state

On a build that predates the first reported backtest, the share of capital in use varied through time. A study compared a cash yield on idle capital with a broad index position: the index added market exposure, while a cash yield added a modest return without changing exposure. I have not settled on a treatment.

Open question

What capital should do when the strategy is not using it: stay in cash, earn a cash yield, or fund a second use that returns it on demand.

Why it matters

It sets the return on total capital and the fairness of any comparison with an always-invested benchmark.

Early hypothesis

Two remedies look tempting, and I hold neither. Putting more of the idle capital behind whatever signals are open is concentration, not utilization: a scarce signal sized larger is a larger bet on the same thing. An index position is market exposure under another name.

Planned approach

A planned extension is to remeasure idle capital on the selected configuration with a financing charge and a cash yield added to the backtest ledger together. I am evaluating whether a second, weakly correlated use could take the idle fraction and return it on demand.

What would change my view

If idle spells proved short or unpredictable, a cash yield would be the simpler answer. A treatment that added return without adding market exposure or deepening the drawdown would be worth considering; an index position does both.
