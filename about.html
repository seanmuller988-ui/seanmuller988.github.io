What I work on

Most of my time goes into one systematic equity strategy and the platform around it: a market-data store, a research replay harness, a transaction-cost model, a verification layer and a trading engine. The project page describes it.

The questions I find most interesting are about evidence: whether a result is real, what it would take to show that it is not, and how much a given sample can resolve. The case studies are where that shows, and the research agenda lists the questions I want to answer next.

Why I built the platform

Much of the platform grew out of questions I wanted to answer rather than features I planned to build. I wanted to see the whole research lifecycle at first hand: data, hypothesis, test, cost, portfolio, order, and the gap between what a simulation says and what a trading engine does.

Building it took me through each of those stages. It also showed me how much depth there is in execution, market microstructure and portfolio construction.

Because this is independent research rather than work produced inside an established research organization, I put particular emphasis on explicit validation, documentation and adversarial testing. I treat the platform as both a research system and a laboratory for improving my own process.

How I approach learning

My preferred way to learn is through research problems with concrete consequences. I usually begin with a question, work out the simplest defensible test I can, and then spend more time trying to invalidate the result than trying to defend it. I learned that order on this project: the audit that found hindsight in my first reported backtest ran after it went out, not before. When the work exposes a gap in my statistics, mathematics, market knowledge or engineering, that becomes the next thing I study.

The part I enjoy most is working out why a convincing result broke under a stronger test. The case studies are examples, and in one of them the explanation that broke was my own.

How the work is done

I independently direct the research, quantitative methodology, experimental design and system architecture, and I use modern AI-assisted development tools extensively for implementation, documentation and iteration.

Because much of the implementation is AI-assisted, and I review results rather than every line of code, the work is only as reliable as its controls, and a control is useful only if it can fail: placebos, parity proofs, plans written before results, and independent re-checks of anything that looks too good. Those checks do not replace outside review. The work has not yet had independent outside review.

What I am working to get better at

My current focus is on strengthening the statistical, execution and portfolio-level foundations of the work.

  • Statistical inference for research with many trials and dependent data
  • Market microstructure and execution, beyond textbook cost models
  • Portfolio construction and risk at the level of the whole book
  • Research systems that stay reproducible as they grow

I am particularly interested in learning from researchers with deeper experience in these areas.
