How I work

What is mine, and what is tooling.

The research questions, the mathematical reasoning, the validation standards and every adopt-or-reject decision are mine. Implementation was substantially AI-assisted: AI coding agents wrote most of the code, the test scaffolding and first drafts of reports, under my direction and review. I do not present code volume as hand-written work.

I think that division is worth stating plainly, because it is also where the judgment shows. Directing a large amount of generated work only helps if you insist on controls that can fail: placebos, parity proofs, pre-registered tests and independent re-verification of anything that looks too good.

Research notes

Short accounts of things that changed my mind.

When the placebo wins

A change to how the tradable universe was screened looked promising: more trades at the same quality. Random versions with the same mechanics and no information scored as well or better. The change was withdrawn, and the lesson kept: a result is not a result until its placebo has run.

Measuring the ceiling first

Several models tried to rank individual signals and none beat chance. Instead of trying a tenth, I measured how much skill was achievable: the true spread between signals was a small fraction of per-trade noise. Two years of data could not resolve it. The work moved to breadth and cost.

A negative result

The project began as an options thesis. It was tested against acceptance gates set in advance, failed them, and was dropped in favour of the equity strategy. The gates were not lowered to save it.
