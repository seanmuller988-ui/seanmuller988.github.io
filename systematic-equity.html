The research platform

Systematic equity research platform

A systematic U.S. equity strategy that trades relative-value effects among single stocks, under portfolio-level risk controls.

The platform has progressed from research and simulation into live trading with real capital through broker-integrated execution. Every performance figure on this site is a historical backtest. No live or paper-trading results are shown here. Signal definitions, parameters and trading rules are omitted from this site.

Overview

The question behind this work is whether relative-value effects among U.S. single stocks are worth trading after realistic costs, and how much of an apparent result survives testing. The economics are easy to state and hard to measure: a stock that moves away from comparable stocks may be under temporary price pressure, or it may have been repriced for a reason that lasts, and the two are hard to tell apart when the move happens. Costs then decide whether the difference is worth trading. My independent research in systematic equities began in May 2025. Answering this question took more than a backtest: every test needed point-in-time data, a replay that could be repeated exactly, a cost model, and a way to count everything that had been tried.

This page documents one configuration of that strategy, which is long-only, together with the research platform around it. The wider research program has also tested short-side variants, none of which is part of the documented configuration, and regime coverage is an open item on the research agenda. The platform covers the full path from market data to order instructions, shown in the research architecture below; the broker connection itself is handled by a separate order service that the trading engine integrates with.

The statistical tools are described on the quantitative methods page, and how the work is done is described on the About page.

Research process

The general loop and the standards behind it are on the methods page. The six steps below are the standard each study in the October 2026 research sweep was run under.

  1. Pre-register. Hypothesis, standing baseline, placebo, sample split and stop rule are written before a study’s report. The record is internal and self-dated: for 16 of the 18 sweep studies, version control shows a short entry with the hypothesis, baseline and placebo before the report.
  2. Baseline. Every variant is judged against the standing baseline, not against zero.
  3. Placebo. A version with the same mechanics and no information must fail. If it does not, the variant earns no credit.
  4. Era split. Choices are set on the first half of the sample and judged on the second, inside the same two-year window; evidence on data that no selection has touched accrues under the forward-evidence program (research agenda).
  5. Same-build replay. Variant and reference run on one pinned build, so a difference is not a build difference.
  6. Family-wise check. Leads with a per-session return series are tested together as one family, with placebo twins as a control family.

From the first reported figure to the restated one

In October 2026 I had my first reported backtest audited and rebuilt.

The audit asked one question of every input: was it knowable on the date it was used? The list of tradable stocks was not. It had been read once from a 2026 snapshot and applied to every earlier date. Trading cost was also a flat charge with every order filled in full.

The universe was rebuilt as of each date under a point-in-time listing and minimum-liquidity rule, and a fill model was added. The effect was sized in sequence; only the endpoints are shown here.

What the rebuild showed. Under the point-in-time universe, most of what fell was breadth: fewer simulated trades. On this evidence the hindsight sat mainly in the list of names. That finding is what led to the revised liquidity requirement, which restores breadth while every order still pays modeled costs.

Key limitations

How to read the headline: 3.04× is a simulated result from the selected configuration, and I present it as where the research stands, not as an expected return.

  • Selection and evaluation overlap. The selected configuration’s liquidity requirement was chosen on the same two-year window it is evaluated on, so the result is not independent out-of-sample evidence. Its deflated Sharpe ratio, a probability and not a reduced Sharpe, is 0.85.
  • The sample is short. One two-year window in one broad market environment, in which the S&P 500 rose about 42% on a price basis. A two-year compounding multiple is sensitive to small changes, and a small fraction of trades accounts for more than half of net profit.
  • Costs are modeled and are being refined with live evidence. The cost model is textbook, with its impact setting conservative under one estimate and about central under the other; execution data from live operation are now being accumulated to refine it. Financing on exposure above equity is not charged.
  • Where the performance originates is still being characterized. The documented configuration is long-only, so part of the result is market return. Factor and benchmark decomposition remain part of the ongoing evidence-extension program used to characterize where the historical performance originates: the first reported trade record has been decomposed, and the selected configuration is being extended the same way.
  • The point-in-time reconstruction is still being completed. Candidates are drawn from the present-day list of names, so names that left that list are never candidates; dividends and later-delisted names sit outside the ledger and, measured separately, add a few percent of net profit.

The full set of qualifications, including the placebo test of the revised liquidity screen, the sensitivity of the multiple to small changes, the start-date test and the trial recount, is in the methodology and validation brief (PDF) and in the case studies.

Validation

What was checked, one line each. The case studies and the methods page carry the detail.

  • The stock universe was audited for hindsight and rebuilt as of each date. The result was restated lower. Case study
  • The trial count behind the deflated Sharpe ratio was recounted. About 540 runs in 62 hypothesis families, where 100 had been assumed. Case study
  • One research sweep was judged once, as a family. Sixty-nine candidates beside 44 placebo twins. One survived: an effect already known, and fragile. Method
  • The power of those tests was measured. Planted effects of two realistic sizes were found about a fifth and about a third of the time, so most nulls in that sweep mean below resolution, not absent. Method
  • The ceiling on ranking skill was measured before more models were built. It was too low for this sample, and that line of modeling was closed. Case study
  • A look-ahead audit was found to share the defect it checked for. Every earlier pass from it was treated as void, and the rule since is that a check must not share code with what it checks.
  • The research fork had to reproduce the reference backtest before any experiment ran on it. It did, to the dollar, and the parity checker correctly failed a build with a known rounding change.

Which of these checks has been applied to which backtest, and the cases themselves, are written up in the methodology and validation brief (PDF).

Risk

A risk check sits between every proposed entry and the order path.

By design, portfolio-level risk controls hold a veto over new positions. A long-only relative-value book is not hedged: besides the stock-specific move it was entered for, each position carries market exposure and the common exposure it shares with comparable stocks, so positions that each pass on their own can share one exposure and lose together. That is why the risk check looks at the whole book and not only at the trade in front of it.

The same exposure distorts tests. On a long-only book in a rising market, a test that shortens holding periods and is scored on raw return is biased toward longer holds, because shortening gives up market drift. One such verdict is behind the standard on scoring net of market exposure.

The specific limits and triggers are omitted from this site.

What it lets the research do

  • A ledger of runs. Launch logs record a backtest run with its build and its configuration, and the ledger assembled from them and from earlier queue records in October 2026 is where the trial count for multiple-testing corrections comes from. It was built by recount, not kept from the start, and result files survive for only part of it.
  • Replay of the real-time loop. The loop that trades in real time can be run over completed historical bars under a frozen clock, which is how the replay is compared with the real-time record.
  • One market-data store. By design, research and trading read the same bars through a reader role that cannot write.

Working within limits

The research has run on about two years of intraday history and shared hardware with limited memory. One case where a shortage shaped the design of a test.

  • One unseen month, spent onceFor one separate research line, one month of data was available that no earlier analysis had read.The pass and fail bars were committed before any row of that month was read, together with a rule that capped the verdict at underpowered if the minimum detectable effect exceeded the effect seen in the earlier sample. The minimum detectable effect was computed and reported first, ahead of the result.The month could be read once without being over-read: the rule fired, the verdict was recorded as underpowered and descriptive only, and a more flattering variant was left out because one of its components had failed a reproduction check set in advance. An adjacent month, read outside the pre-registered test, disagreed.

Engineering detail

  • Environments. Research, development, staging and production, each with its own trade database.
  • Releases. Since mid-September 2026 a release candidate is a tagged commit with a generated manifest of component versions and, since late September, a frozen set of governed configuration values. The built artifact is checksummed. Code is promoted; state is not copied between environments.
  • Release gate. Each release candidate is replayed against a fixed baseline over a small fixed set of sessions, and every difference in decisions must match a declared one. After one build passed on an empty comparison file, a third verdict, inconclusive, was added for empty or unattested evidence.
  • Data discipline. Minute bars are stored as traded, with corporate actions held as separate records.
