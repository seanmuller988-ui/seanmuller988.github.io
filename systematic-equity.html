The project

Systematic Equity platform

A systematic U.S. equity research and trading platform focused on relative-value opportunities, portfolio-level risk controls, realistic execution and continuous validation.

The platform has progressed from research and simulation into live trading with real capital through broker-integrated execution. Every performance figure on this site is a historical backtest. No live or paper-trading results are shown here. Signal definitions, parameters and trading rules are omitted from this site.

Overview

What exists, and what this page does and does not show.

The platform covers the full path from market data to orders: a minute-resolution data warehouse; a research replay harness; a trading engine; a verification layer set up to test, stage by stage, whether the trading engine matches the research reference; portfolio and risk controls; an order path through a separate order service to a retail broker's API; and a release process from research through staging to production. The order service is outside this code base, and it is not my work.

This page describes the system, research process and results at the level a reviewer needs to judge the work. Proprietary signal construction, feature definitions, thresholds, portfolio rules and execution logic are intentionally omitted.

How the work is done is described on the About page.

How a change reaches the trading engine

This is the route by design. The headline figures on this page come from the research replay harness: they are backtest results, not results of the trading engine.

  1. A research question is written down with its baseline, its placebo and its stop rule before the study's report. The record is internal and self-dated.
  2. The change is built as an opt-in patch, in nearly all cases behind a switch that is off by default. For the patches added in the October sweep the off path is recorded as byte-identical, and every experiment is compared with a reference run on the same build.
  3. The replay harness runs the variant and its control on the same build and the same data.
  4. Results are judged against the placebo and, across a sweep, in one family-wise test.
  5. Only an adopted change is promoted: a tagged release with a manifest of component versions and a frozen set of governed configuration values.
  6. By design a release goes to staging before it is eligible for production.

Research process

The six steps of the October 2026 research sweep.

1 · Pre-register

Hypothesis, standing baseline, placebo, sample split and stop rule are written before a study's report. The record is internal and self-dated: for 16 of the 18 sweep studies, version control shows a short entry with the hypothesis, baseline and placebo before the report; the longer pre-registration files entered version control together with their reports.

2 · Baseline

Every variant is judged against the standing baseline, not against zero.

3 · Placebo

A version with the same mechanics and no information must fail. If it does not, the variant earns no credit.

4 · Era split

Choices are set on the first half of the sample and judged on the second, inside the same two-year window. No untouched holdout exists.

5 · Same-build replay

Variant and reference run on one pinned build, so a difference is not a build difference.

6 · Family-wise check

Leads with a per-session return series are tested together as one family, with placebo twins as a control family.

From the first reported figure to the restated one

In October 2026 I had my first reported backtest audited and rebuilt.

The audit asked one question of every input: was it knowable on the date it was used? The list of tradable stocks was not. It had been read once from a 2026 snapshot and applied to every earlier date. Trading cost was also a flat charge with every order filled in full.

The universe was rebuilt as of each date under a point-in-time listing and minimum-liquidity rule, and a fill model was added. The effect was sized in sequence; only the endpoints are shown here.

That finding is what led to the revised liquidity requirement, which restores breadth while every order still pays modeled costs.

Limits that matter

How to read the headline: 3.04× is a simulated result from the selected configuration, which was chosen on the same two years it is measured on, and it has not itself passed the six steps above: its liquidity requirement was chosen on the full window, and the revised screen did not beat its placebos. There is no untouched holdout, the deflated Sharpe ratio of 0.85 is below the conventional 0.95 bar, and a gap of about 0.5× between two simulations is inside the noise. I present it as where the research stands, not as an expected return.

  • The headline is one implementation of one two-year window. Its deflated Sharpe ratio, a probability and not a reduced Sharpe, is 0.85 against the 240 full-window simulations on file. That count is simulations on file, not ideas tried, and may understate the search: the recount below found about 540 runs.
  • The minimum-liquidity requirement was chosen after testing five settings on this window. Every revised setting beat the original after costs, and several settings are within noise of one another.
  • The first of the two revisions, the revised liquidity screen, did not pass its placebo control. Placebo versions of the screen, with its information replaced by a random draw and the original requirement kept, mostly finished level with the real one or above it, before and after costs, and ended in the same range as the revised requirement settings. I credit that revision with no skill of its own, and part of the gap between 1.87× and 3.04× comes from it.
  • A compounding two-year multiple moves by roughly ±0.5× under small, arbitrary changes, even though the simulation itself is deterministic. Start date can move a result by more: on the first reported backtest the reported start date was the best of five nearby starts tested, and the other four ended lower by more than that band. The test has not yet been repeated on the selected configuration.
  • Fitted parameters are out of sample in a walk-forward test, but there is no purge or embargo gap between fitting data and test data, and the choice among configurations used the whole window.
  • Profit is concentrated. A small fraction of trades accounts for more than half of net profit.
  • The window ends inside a drawdown: the final mark is about 18% below the peak.
  • The historical simulation can use leverage, and financing cost on exposure above the account's equity is not charged.
  • Over the same window the S&P 500 rose about 42% on a price basis, with a maximum drawdown of about 19%. That is context, not a like-for-like comparison. Market exposure has not been separated from the result: factor attribution on the selected configuration is pending.
  • Dividends and names that later delisted are outside the simulation ledger. Measured separately on the original configuration, they add a few percent of net profit.
  • The point-in-time rebuild is partial. Candidates are still drawn from the present-day list of names, so names that left that list are never candidates. One intermediate model input has not yet been rebuilt as of date, and corporate-action adjustments are not applied as of date. A test with a wider set of candidate names, run on a modified configuration, ended lower after modeled costs.

Validation

What was checked, and what the checks found.

Sweep-wide inference

Sixty-nine candidate improvements entered one correlation-aware family-wise test with a block bootstrap over sessions. Forty-four placebo twins of the selection-type candidates were tested as a control family. One candidate survived on the whole sample at the study's primary block length. It was an effect already known, it did not survive at the longest block lengths, and it did not survive in the reporting era (the later half of the sample) alone. The placebo panel behaved as a null should. The sweep took the first reported backtest (superseded: hindsight universe, flat cost) as its reference, and it has not been repeated on the point-in-time universe with modeled costs.

Trial accounting

The earlier results packet deflated its Sharpe ratio for 100 trials. A recount found about 540 simulation runs in 62 families over the same two-year period, some on shifted starts or part of the window, with highly correlated returns. By a simple Bonferroni correction the first-reported Sharpe (superseded) survives at the 5% level for up to 55 independent trials, so it does not survive at the 100 that packet assumed. The correction is reported as a range because the runs are highly correlated.

Power of the tests

Known effects of two realistic sizes were planted into the candidates' measured correlation structure. At the studies' own thresholds they were found about a fifth and about a third of the time. The sample, not the threshold, limited discovery, so most nulls in this record mean below resolution, not absent.

Noise ceiling

Before building more models to rank individual signals, the achievable skill was measured. The true spread between signals is more than ten times smaller than the noise in a single signal's outcome, so even a ranker that knew the truth would reach only a very low rank correlation. The feasibility rule written into the study's pre-registration required a higher ceiling than was found, and that line of modeling was closed as below resolution.

An audit that shared its defect

In September 2026 a look-ahead in the walk-forward calendar passed the look-ahead audit, because the audit used the same date-handling call as the code it checked. Every earlier pass from that audit was treated as void, one fix corrected both, and results were measured again on the corrected base. The rule since: a check must not share code with what it checks. A deliberate leak-injection test of the audit has not yet been run.

Parity before experiments

Before any study ran, the unpatched research fork reproduced a same-day re-run of the reference, the first reported backtest (superseded), to the dollar, and the parity checker correctly failed a build with a known rounding change. The patched build used for the experiments, with every switch off, made the same trades and ended about fifty dollars away, a rounding difference inherited from earlier builds. Variants are therefore compared only with a reference run on their own build. This is a lineage proof on one code path, not an independent replication.

Risk

A risk check sits between every proposed entry and the order path.

By design, a portfolio-level risk governor holds a veto over new positions.

A habit that came out of this work is asking what a control actually measures: in the early operational failure described under Infrastructure, every existing check passed because every check measured entries. The specific limits and triggers are omitted from this site.

Infrastructure

Stated at the strength the system actually enforces.

  • Environments. Research, development, staging and production, each with its own trade database. Market data is one shared store whose reader role cannot write.
  • Releases. Since mid-September 2026 a release candidate is a tagged commit with a generated manifest of component versions and, since late September, a frozen set of governed configuration values. The built artifact is checksummed, not cryptographically signed. Code is promoted; state is not copied between environments.
  • Release gate. Each release candidate is replayed against a fixed baseline over a small fixed set of sessions, and every difference in decisions must match a declared one. After one build passed on an empty comparison file, a third verdict, inconclusive, was added for empty or unattested evidence. Declared differences that the replay cannot produce are marked as not yet provable.
  • Checks. A local commit hook runs lint and strict type checks on the files being committed.
  • Data discipline. Minute bars are stored as traded, with corporate actions held as separate records. Two limits are open: adjustments are not yet applied as of each date, and the store holds no intraday history for names that later delisted.
  • Incidents. An early operational failure that every existing check missed, because every check measured entries, led to a verification layer built on numeric parity proofs. A queue race that exhausted memory on the research host led to a start lock and a hard concurrency cap.
