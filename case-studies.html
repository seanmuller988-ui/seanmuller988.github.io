Data · October 2026

Restating a backtest whose stock universe was built with hindsight

My first reported backtest was walk-forward for its models, but its list of tradable stocks had been read once from a present-day snapshot. An audit I asked for found it. Rebuilding the universe as of each date, and then modeling fills, took the two-year backtest result from a superseded 4.76× to 1.87×.

Market hypothesis · July 2026

Testing my own hypothesis about crowded chart triggers until no trading claim was left

My hypothesis was that textbook chart triggers on crowded instruments produce predictable reactions. Most early hits were one market day counted once per instrument, and an intraday screen passed 38 of 48 cells against a placebo that ignored the move. The control that fixed that had a look-ahead of its own.

Statistics · July 2026

Selecting on a noisy proxy: a result that looked real and was measurement error

A rule that bought when a model-based proxy for a price read low passed significance, dose-response, month consistency and placebos. Selecting on the previous day’s reading removed it, and actual quotes removed it again. The usual battery cannot see an error shared by the selection and the price paid.

Market hypothesis · August 2026

Reverse splits faked a short-side result; the repaired result was rejected on its tail

I asked whether extreme gainers give their gains back. The top of the first ranking was mostly reverse stock splits, an error that pointed the way of the hypothesis. On repaired data the average was positive. The idea was still rejected, because the worst-case criterion had been fixed before measurement.

Counting the trials behind a deflated Sharpe ratio

My first reported backtest deflated its Sharpe ratio for an assumed 100 trials. A recount found about 540 runs in 62 hypothesis families. The claim was restated as a range, and the next research sweep was judged once, as a family, beside placebo twins.

Trading rule · 2026

An entry filter designed around a set of losing trades did no better than random filters on earlier data

A filter proposed after a post-mortem on a set of losing trades looked like a clear improvement on the trades it was designed around. Scored on earlier simulated trades with only information known before entry, it blocked the better trades, and the trades it kept did no better than random filters of the same size.
