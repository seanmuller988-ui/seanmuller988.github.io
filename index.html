Selected configuration: backtest results

Historical backtest · selected configuration: two-year walk-forward, point-in-time universe, modeled transaction costs, $1M simulated start.

3.04×backtest ending multiple, Jun 2024 – Jul 2026 (about 70% a year)
1.84backtest Sharpe ratio on daily returns, annualized
−19.1%backtest maximum drawdown on daily marks
780simulated trades; about 40 basis points modeled cost per round trip

Selected configuration result. Configuration selection and evaluation overlap in this research window, so it is not independent out-of-sample evidence. Deflated Sharpe ratio: 0.85. That ratio is a probability, not a reduced Sharpe, and 0.85 is below the conventional 0.95 bar. The backtest of the original configuration under the same assumptions ends at 1.87×. Selected case studies

Post-mortems from the research record: what I believed, what showed it was incomplete, and what changed.

Data

Restating a backtest whose stock universe was built with hindsight

My first reported backtest was walk-forward for its models, but its list of tradable stocks had been read once from a present-day snapshot. An audit I asked for found it. Rebuilding the universe as of each date, and then modeling fills, took the two-year backtest result from a superseded 4.76× to 1.87×.

Execution

Replacing a flat cost assumption with a modeled fill

The first reported backtest charged a flat cost and filled every order in full. A fill model with spread, impact, delay and partial fills lowered the result, and showed that an ending multiple cannot rank cost assumptions.

Statistics

Counting the trials behind a deflated Sharpe ratio

My first reported backtest deflated its Sharpe ratio for an assumed 100 trials. A recount found about 540 runs in 62 families. The claim was restated as a range, and the next research sweep was judged once, as a family, beside placebo twins.
