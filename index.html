The research at a glance

What I study

Cross-sectional and relative-value behavior in U.S. equities. I test whether deviations from comparable stocks are temporary or persistent, whether they remain meaningful after costs, and whether the evidence survives point-in-time reconstruction and robustness checks.

What I built

An end-to-end environment spanning point-in-time data, walk-forward replay, statistical validation, portfolio construction, execution modeling, broker-integrated trading, risk controls, and monitoring.

How I work

I pre-specify hypotheses and failure criteria, respect time in evaluation, use dependence-aware inference and placebo controls, account for multiple testing, and write post-mortems when the evidence goes against me.

Selected case studies

Data

Restating a backtest built with hindsight

An audit of my first reported backtest found that its list of tradable stocks had been read from a present-day snapshot; rebuilt as of each date, the result was restated lower.

Walk-forward testing had protected the fitted parameters, not the universe.

Market hypothesis

A market hypothesis of my own, tested to rejection

I expected widely watched chart triggers on heavily traded instruments to produce predictable reactions; three controls removed every tradable claim.

A count of events is not a count of observations when the instruments share a market.

Statistics

Selecting on a noisy proxy

A buy rule keyed to a model-based price proxy passed dose-response, period splits and placebos; the effect was measurement error shared by the selection and the price it was scored against.

The standard battery is blind to a shared error by construction.

How I test whether a result is real

A result counts only after it survives the ways a backtest can mislead. I rebuild inputs point-in-time, evaluate forward through time, use dependence-aware inference and placebo controls, count multiple trials, test statistical power, and model transaction costs before treating an effect as credible.
